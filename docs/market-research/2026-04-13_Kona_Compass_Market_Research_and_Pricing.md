## Executive Summary

Kona Compass operates in a real and growing premium niche: **control systems and rotator accessories for forward-facing/live sonar users**.

This is not a mass-market fishing accessory category. It is a **high-intent, high-ticket equipment segment** serving anglers who already invest heavily in electronics such as:
- Garmin Panoptix LiveScope
- Lowrance ActiveTarget / ActiveTarget 2
- Humminbird MEGA Live

This matters because the target customer has already accepted premium hardware pricing. The market data and competitor review show that buyers in this segment routinely pay:
- around **€330** for manual mounts,
- around **€1,100 to $1,200** for motorized systems,
- around **€1,700** for advanced automated systems,
- around **$2,500 to $2,800** for premium integrated aiming systems.

Based on the currently documented Kona Compass feature set, the product should **not** be positioned as a budget accessory. It belongs in the **serious mid-premium tier**.

**Recommended launch MSRP:**
- **€1,190** for Europe
- **$1,290** for USA/Canada

**Recommended launch offer / early-adopter price:**
- **€990 to €1,090**
- **$1,090 to $1,190**

This positioning keeps Kona Compass:
- above the “cheap / DIY / uncertain” zone,
- competitive with established motorized systems,
- clearly below the ultra-premium tier,
- credible for dealers and early adopters.

## 3. Market Structure

## 3.1 Category definition
Kona Compass sits in the segment of:

**forward-facing sonar accessories / live sonar transducer control systems**

This category includes:
- manual rotators
- motorized rotators
- automated search systems
- aiming systems with foot control
- transducer control accessories integrated with trolling motors or poles

## 3.2 Why this category is commercially attractive
Customers in this category have already accepted expensive electronics setups.

Typical sonar hardware pricing from current market references shows that anglers already pay roughly:
- **$1,399 to $1,699** for Lowrance ActiveTarget systems,
- around **$1,600** for Humminbird MEGA Live class products,
- around **$1,800+** for Garmin LiveScope Plus bundles,
- more when paired with premium chartplotters, mounts, or trolling motors.

That means a precision control accessory in the **$1,000 to $1,500** range is commercially plausible.

## 4. Regional Market Readiness

## 4.1 United States
The USA is the strongest priority market.

Reasons:
- high adoption of forward-facing sonar,
- dense ecosystem of bass, crappie, and walleye anglers,
- strong dealer network for marine electronics,
- established culture of paying for performance accessories,
- strong presence of community influence via anglers, forums, YouTube, Facebook groups, and tournaments.

Forward-facing sonar has become mainstream inside the serious electronics-driven angling segment in the USA.

## 4.2 Canada
Canada is a strong adjacent market.

Reasons:
- predator and walleye fishing relevance,
- overlap with US product awareness,
- similar premium equipment buyer behavior,
- opportunity to sell through dealers and direct online channels.

## 4.3 Scandinavia
Scandinavia is a highly promising premium niche.

Reasons:
- technically mature anglers,
- strong predator fishing culture,
- good fit for engineering-led premium positioning,
- strong brand receptiveness to rugged, no-nonsense equipment.

This region is likely better suited to Kona Compass than generic broad-Europe messaging.

## 4.4 Europe beyond Scandinavia
Europe is viable, but fragmented.

A practical rollout order would be:
1. USA
2. Canada
3. Scandinavia
4. Germany / Netherlands / UK
5. broader Europe later

## 7. Price Positioning Analysis for Kona Compass

## 7.1 Why Kona Compass should not be cheap
A low price would create the wrong market signal.

In this category, “too cheap” often reads as:
- DIY-grade,
- unreliable,
- unfinished,
- unsupported,
- risky on the water,
- inferior to established systems.

For sonar electronics buyers, confidence matters more than bargain optics.

- **€890 to €1,190**
